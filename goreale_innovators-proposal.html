1. Project Title

Goreale Agricultural Innovators: Activating an Existing Digital Agricultural Platform for Community Engagement, Farmer Information and Climate-Smart Innovation.

2. Executive Summary

Goreale Agricultural Innovators is a youth-led agricultural innovation initiative established to address practical challenges affecting farmers and agricultural communities in Goreale and surrounding areas of Kenya.

The initiative is led by Samira Abdirahman Maalim, who is applying as an individual innovator while coordinating a broader youth-led initiative. The project seeks to use technology and community participation to improve access to agricultural information, communication, knowledge sharing and sustainable farming practices.

An important milestone has already been achieved: the initiative's online platform has been developed and implemented. This means the proposal is not simply requesting funding to develop an idea or create a website. A digital foundation already exists.

The existing platform is intended to serve as a formal digital communication and information-sharing channel between the initiative and participating farmers and community stakeholders. It provides a foundation for organizing agricultural information, communicating updates and supporting engagement with the target community.

However, having an online platform alone does not guarantee meaningful community impact. The next challenge is to activate it, introduce it to intended users, test its usefulness, improve its practical application and establish reliable connections between digital communication and real agricultural activities.

Through the Mekatilili Micro-Grant, I am requesting KES 100,000 to undertake a focused three-month pilot. The project will concentrate on platform activation, community onboarding, farmer engagement, agricultural information testing, feedback collection and evaluation.

The microgrant will support a defined implementation milestone. It will not be presented as sufficient capital for the entire long-term development of the initiative. Additional investment will eventually be required to establish a physical operational office, strengthen technical infrastructure, expand field activities and support wider implementation.

3. Background and Existing Progress

Agricultural communities face interconnected challenges in accessing timely information, communicating agricultural needs, adopting efficient farming methods and responding to changing environmental conditions.

Digital technology can help bridge the information gap, but many proposed digital solutions remain at the idea stage without a functioning platform or a practical community-testing process.

Goreale Agricultural Innovators has already taken an important step by developing and implementing an online platform. This represents the initiative's initial technological foundation and demonstrates progress beyond the conceptual stage.

Progress already achieved

  • An online platform has been developed and implemented.
  • A digital foundation for agricultural communication exists.
  • The initiative has established its direction around agricultural innovation and community needs.
  • A youth-led implementation approach has been established.
  • The next development stage has been identified as community activation, testing and improvement.
Current innovation stage: The platform already exists. The proposed grant will help move it from an implemented digital foundation towards demonstrated community use and practical validation.

4. Problem Statement

Farmers and agricultural communities require accessible, reliable and practical information to make informed agricultural decisions.

However, information gaps and limited coordination can make it difficult for farmers to access useful guidance, share challenges and engage with agricultural innovation.

The initiative has identified the following challenges as priorities for community assessment and pilot testing:

  • Limited access to timely agricultural information.
  • Communication gaps between farmers and available agricultural information providers.
  • Limited access to practical information on water management and irrigation.
  • Crop-pest identification and management challenges.
  • Limited exposure to climate-smart agricultural practices.
  • Difficulty coordinating community agricultural activities.
  • Limited opportunities for youth to apply digital skills to local agricultural challenges.

Although an online platform has already been implemented, its practical value must be demonstrated through real community engagement, user feedback and measurable implementation results.

5. The Innovation Approach

The innovation is based on connecting an existing digital platform with practical community engagement rather than treating technology as an isolated solution.

The project will use a digital-to-community innovation model in which the online platform supports communication, while direct engagement with farmers ensures that the information and services remain relevant to local needs.

5.1 Existing Digital Platform

The existing online platform will serve as the central digital communication and information-sharing point for the initiative. During the pilot, the platform will be tested and improved according to user experience and identified community needs.

5.2 Formal Communication Channel

The platform will function as the initiative's formal digital communication channel. It will provide an organized way to share project updates, agricultural information, community notices and relevant engagement opportunities.

5.3 Human-Centred Community Testing

Farmers will be engaged directly to understand whether the platform is accessible, understandable and useful. Their feedback will guide improvements rather than assuming that technology automatically solves agricultural challenges.

5.4 Digital and Physical Integration

The project will connect online communication with real-world agricultural engagement. Digital information will be complemented by farmer discussions, practical demonstrations where feasible, community feedback and field observations.

5.5 Evidence-Driven Improvement

User engagement, platform activity, farmer feedback and pilot observations will be documented. The findings will guide the next stage of development and help identify which services should be prioritized for expansion.

Core innovation: An already implemented digital agricultural platform combined with human-centred testing, local communication and practical community participation to develop a replicable agricultural information model.

6. Project Goal

To activate, test and strengthen an existing digital agricultural platform through a three-month community pilot that improves agricultural communication, access to useful information and youth-led innovation in Goreale and surrounding communities.

7. Specific Objectives

  1. Activate the existing online platform for structured agricultural communication and community engagement.
  2. Introduce the platform to an initial group of farmers and intended community users.
  3. Test the accessibility, relevance and usefulness of digital agricultural information.
  4. Identify farmer information needs relating to irrigation, crop-pest management and climate-smart agriculture.
  5. Establish a practical feedback mechanism through which users can communicate challenges and suggestions.
  6. Improve the platform based on actual user experience.
  7. Document pilot findings and develop a clear pathway for further investment and expansion.

14. Existing Infrastructure and Long-Term Capital Needs

Existing Online Infrastructure

The initiative has already developed and implemented its online platform. This existing infrastructure reduces the need to spend the microgrant on creating a digital product from the beginning.

The proposed investment will instead focus on activating the existing platform, testing it with intended users, improving its functionality and establishing effective community communication.

The Need for a Physical Office

As the initiative grows, a dedicated physical office will become important for coordinating operations, organizing farmer engagement, maintaining project records, supporting technical activities and providing a reliable point of contact for community stakeholders.

A physical office is therefore part of the initiative's long-term institutional development needs. It is not being presented as an expense to be financed through this KES 100,000 microgrant.

Need for Additional Implementation Capital

The microgrant will finance a limited but important implementation milestone. Full deployment of the initiative will require considerably more capital than the microgrant provides.

Future investment priorities include:

  • Establishing and equipping a dedicated physical office.
  • Strengthening technical infrastructure and platform capacity.
  • Expanding community access and farmer onboarding.
  • Supporting wider agricultural field demonstrations.
  • Developing additional agricultural information services.
  • Establishing stronger operational and partnership capacity.
  • Expanding the model to additional farming communities.
Funding strategy: The Mekatilili Micro-Grant will support a focused, measurable pilot. The results will provide evidence for attracting additional investment and building the infrastructure required for full implementation.

15. Innovation, Learning and Scalability

The project is innovative in its implementation approach: rather than investing first in a completely new technology, it begins with an existing platform and tests how that platform can create practical value for agricultural communities.

The three-month pilot will test important assumptions:

  1. Whether farmers find the platform useful.
  2. Whether digital communication improves access to relevant agricultural information.
  3. Which agricultural topics users need most.
  4. Which communication methods encourage participation.
  5. What technical improvements are necessary.
  6. What resources are required for expansion.

The findings will determine the next development priorities. The platform can subsequently be improved and extended to additional communities using lessons from actual user experience rather than assumptions.

16. Sustainability

The initiative's sustainability approach is based on building on existing digital infrastructure, strengthening community relationships and gradually expanding its capacity.

  • Maintaining the existing online platform.

  • Encouraging youth participation and technical learning.
  • Using pilot evidence to guide future investment.
  • Exploring partnerships for technical and agricultural support.
