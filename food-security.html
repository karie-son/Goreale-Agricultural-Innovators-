Creating a Food-Secure Future

Food security exists when all people, at all times, have physical and economic access to sufficient, safe and nutritious food that meets their dietary needs and food preferences for an active and healthy life.

Achieving food security requires more than increasing agricultural production. It also involves improving food distribution, household incomes, nutrition, storage, food safety, market access and the ability of communities to withstand droughts and other disruptions.

Agribusiness connects agricultural production with processing, transportation, marketing, distribution and consumption. It creates opportunities for farmers, entrepreneurs, traders, processors and other participants in the agricultural value chain.

Goreale Agricultural Innovators promotes sustainable food systems that support farmers, strengthen local enterprises and improve access to nutritious food.

The Four Pillars of Food Security

Food security is commonly understood through four connected pillars. All four are important for individuals, households and communities.

1. Food Availability

Food must be physically available through local production, livestock, fisheries, storage, trade and distribution.

  • Increasing crop production.
  • Supporting livestock farming.
  • Improving food storage.
  • Reducing post-harvest losses.

2. Food Access

People must be able to obtain food physically and economically. Income, employment, transport, markets and food prices all influence access.

  • Creating agricultural employment.
  • Improving household incomes.
  • Supporting affordable food markets.
  • Strengthening market connections.

3. Food Utilization

Food should be safe, nutritious and properly prepared. Good nutrition, clean water, sanitation and health practices support effective use of food by the body.

  • Promoting dietary diversity.
  • Improving food hygiene.
  • Encouraging safe food preparation.
  • Supporting nutrition education.

4. Food Stability

People should have reliable access to food throughout the year, including during droughts, economic difficulties and other disruptions.

  • Strengthening food reserves.
  • Promoting climate-resilient farming.
  • Diversifying household income.
  • Improving early-warning systems.

Sustainable Food Production

Sustainable food production increases the supply of food while protecting natural resources and maintaining the ability of future generations to produce food.

Crop Production

Growing cereals, legumes, vegetables, fruits and other suitable crops to provide food and income.

Livestock Production

Producing milk, meat, eggs and other livestock products through responsible animal management.

Fish Farming

Developing suitable aquaculture systems to provide fish as a source of food, nutrition and income.

Horticulture

Producing vegetables, fruits and other high-value crops for household consumption and commercial markets.

Agroforestry

Integrating suitable trees with crops and livestock to support food production, soil protection and livelihoods.

Integrated Farming

Combining crops, livestock and other farm enterprises to make better use of available resources.

Food Diversity and Nutrition

Food security is not only about having enough food. It also involves having access to a variety of safe and nutritious foods that support healthy growth, development and wellbeing.

Cereals and Staples

Foods such as maize, millet, sorghum, rice and other grains provide energy and form part of many diets.

Legumes and Pulses

Beans, cowpeas, green grams, lentils and peas provide plant protein, fibre and important nutrients.

Vegetables

Leafy vegetables and other vegetables provide vitamins, minerals and dietary fibre.

Fruits

Fruits contribute vitamins, minerals, fibre and variety to household diets.

Animal-Sourced Foods

Milk, eggs, meat and fish can provide protein and important micronutrients.

Healthy Oils and Fats

Appropriate amounts of healthy oils and fats contribute energy and support the absorption of certain vitamins.

Promoting Household Nutrition

Encourage diverse meals using locally available foods. Safe preparation, clean drinking water, proper food storage and good hygiene are important parts of healthy nutrition.

Food Storage and Post-Harvest Management

Post-harvest management includes all activities carried out after harvesting to protect food quality, reduce losses and prepare agricultural products for consumption or sale.

Proper Grain Drying

Drying grains to appropriate moisture levels helps reduce spoilage and the risk of mould during storage.

Safe Grain Storage

Clean, dry and suitable storage facilities help protect grains from moisture, pests and contamination.

Cold Storage

Refrigeration helps preserve perishable products such as milk, meat, fruits and vegetables.

Food Processing

Processing methods such as milling, drying, pasteurization and packaging can improve product usability and value.

Packaging

Appropriate packaging protects food during handling, transportation and marketing.

Transport and Distribution

Efficient transportation helps move food from farms and processing facilities to consumers and markets.

Reducing Food Losses

  • Harvest crops at the appropriate maturity stage.
  • Handle agricultural products carefully.
  • Dry grains properly before storage.
  • Use suitable storage containers and facilities.
  • Inspect stored products regularly for pests and damage.
  • Maintain the cold chain for perishable foods.
  • Improve transport, packaging and market coordination.

Food Safety and Quality Management

Food safety protects consumers from contamination and foodborne illness. Every stage of the food chain, from production to consumption, requires proper hygiene and responsible handling.

Farm Hygiene

Maintain clean production areas, use safe water and follow appropriate hygiene practices.

Safe Chemical Use

Follow approved product labels, application instructions, protective measures and required waiting periods.

Clean Processing

Clean and maintain equipment, utensils and processing areas to reduce contamination.

Safe Water

Use water that is suitable for food production, processing, cleaning and drinking.

Proper Food Handling

Separate raw and ready-to-eat foods and maintain appropriate storage temperatures.

Quality Standards

Follow relevant food safety requirements and quality standards when producing and selling food.

Agribusiness and Agricultural Enterprise

Agribusiness involves commercial activities connected to agriculture. It includes supplying farm inputs, producing crops and livestock, processing food, transporting products and selling agricultural goods.

Crop Farming Business

Growing and selling cereals, vegetables, fruits and other agricultural products.

Livestock Business

Producing and marketing milk, meat, eggs, breeding animals and other livestock products.

Agro-Processing

Transforming agricultural raw materials into products with improved quality, shelf life or market value.

Farm Input Supply

Selling seeds, fertilisers, animal feeds, tools, equipment and other agricultural inputs.

Agricultural Transport

Providing services for moving agricultural inputs and products between farms, markets and processors.

Agri-Technology Services

Offering digital farming services, irrigation solutions, equipment hire and agricultural information.

Agricultural Value Chains

An agricultural value chain describes the activities and people involved in moving a product from production to the final consumer. Improving coordination between participants can increase efficiency and create more value.

1. Input Supply

Seeds, fertilisers, animal feeds, equipment and other materials needed for agricultural production.

2. Production

Farmers grow crops, raise livestock and produce agricultural raw materials.

3. Aggregation

Agricultural products are collected from different producers for bulk handling and distribution.

4. Processing

Products are cleaned, graded, processed or transformed into other marketable goods.

5. Distribution

Products are transported and supplied to wholesalers, retailers and other buyers.

6. Consumption

Consumers purchase, prepare and use agricultural products as food or other goods.

Agricultural Marketing and Market Access

Agricultural marketing connects producers with customers and helps farmers receive value for their products. Understanding markets allows farmers to make informed production and business decisions.

Important Marketing Practices

  • Identify potential buyers before production where possible.
  • Understand customer preferences and product requirements.
  • Monitor market prices and seasonal demand.
  • Grade and package products appropriately.
  • Maintain reliable product quality and supply.
  • Use cooperatives and producer groups where beneficial.
  • Explore digital platforms and direct marketing opportunities.
  • Keep accurate sales and business records.

Adding Value to Agricultural Products

Value addition can include cleaning, sorting, grading, drying, milling, packaging, processing and branding. These activities may create additional business opportunities when there is sufficient demand and the costs are manageable.

Food Security in Dryland Areas

Dryland communities often depend on rainfall-sensitive crop production and livestock systems. Irregular rainfall, drought, high temperatures and water shortages can affect agricultural production and household food supplies.

Sustainable approaches should combine crop production, livestock management, water conservation, food storage, income diversification and reliable market access.

Drought-Adapted Crops

Promote suitable crops such as sorghum, millet and drought-tolerant varieties of other locally appropriate crops.

Livestock Resilience

Improve livestock feeding, animal health, water access and grazing management.

Water Harvesting

Collect and store rainwater to support farming, livestock and household needs.

Food Storage

Improve grain storage and food preservation to support household supplies beyond the harvest season.

Income Diversification

Support additional income opportunities through agribusiness, livestock products and agricultural services.

Market Connections

Strengthen links between farmers, livestock producers, traders, processors and consumers.

Focus on Garissa and Northern Kenya

Practical food security activities in Garissa and other dryland areas can include:

  • Promoting suitable drought-adapted crops.
  • Supporting pastoral and agro-pastoral livelihoods.
  • Improving livestock health and fodder availability.
  • Developing rainwater harvesting and storage systems.
  • Encouraging household food gardens where water permits.
  • Improving milk handling, storage and marketing.
  • Supporting small agricultural businesses and cooperatives.
  • Promoting early warning and drought preparedness.

Challenges Affecting Food Security

Climate Change

Droughts, floods and changing rainfall patterns can disrupt crop and livestock production.

High Production Costs

Expensive inputs, labour, energy and transportation can affect farmers' profitability.

Post-Harvest Losses

Poor storage, handling and transport can reduce the quantity and quality of available food.

Limited Market Access

Poor infrastructure and weak market connections can prevent farmers from selling products efficiently.

Water Shortages

Insufficient water can limit crop production, livestock survival and household food gardens.

Limited Finance

Lack of affordable financing can restrict investment in farming, storage and processing.

Pests and Diseases

Crop pests, livestock diseases and food contamination can threaten production and food quality.

Food Price Changes

Rising or unstable food prices can reduce household purchasing power and access to nutritious food.

Limited Agricultural Information

Inadequate access to training and reliable information can affect farm productivity and business decisions.

Strategies for Improving Food Security

  1. Increase sustainable production: Improve crop and livestock productivity using appropriate agricultural practices.
  2. Protect natural resources: Improve soil health, water management and environmental conservation.
  3. Strengthen food storage: Reduce post-harvest losses through better handling and storage.
  4. Improve household incomes: Support agricultural businesses, employment and market opportunities.
  5. Promote nutrition education: Encourage diverse diets and safe food preparation.
  6. Develop market infrastructure: Improve transport, storage, processing and distribution systems.
  7. Prepare for emergencies: Strengthen early warning, risk management and community preparedness.
  8. Encourage partnerships: Connect farmers with researchers, government institutions, businesses and development organisations.
