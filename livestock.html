Understanding Livestock Production

Livestock production is an important branch of agriculture involving the rearing and management of domesticated animals for food, income, employment, manure, transport and other economic and social benefits.

It includes the production of milk, meat, eggs, hides, skins, wool and other animal products. Livestock also plays an important role in household food security, rural development and agricultural businesses.

Successful livestock production requires proper animal selection, feeding, breeding, housing, healthcare, hygiene, water management and good animal welfare practices.

Modern livestock farming combines traditional animal husbandry knowledge with veterinary services, improved breeds, balanced nutrition, technology and sustainable production systems.

Goreale Agricultural Innovators promotes responsible livestock production through agricultural education, research, innovation and practical knowledge sharing to improve animal productivity and farmers' livelihoods.

Major Types of Livestock

Livestock includes different animal species kept for food, income, agricultural work and other purposes. Farmers should select animals according to their environment, resources, skills and market opportunities.

Cattle Farming

Cattle are raised for milk, beef, hides, manure and sometimes agricultural work. Production systems include dairy, beef and dual-purpose farming.

Goat Farming

Goats are kept for meat, milk, skins and breeding. They are important in many smallholder and pastoral production systems.

Sheep Farming

Sheep are raised mainly for meat, skins and wool in suitable production environments.

Camel Farming

Camels are important livestock in arid and semi-arid areas, providing milk, meat and transport services.

Poultry Farming

Poultry includes chickens, ducks, turkeys and other domestic birds raised for eggs, meat and income.

Pig Farming

Pigs are raised mainly for meat. Successful production requires suitable housing, nutrition, hygiene and health management.

Rabbit Farming

Rabbits are raised for meat, breeding and other small-scale agricultural business opportunities.

Beekeeping

Beekeeping supports honey and beeswax production while contributing to crop pollination.

Donkeys and Working Animals

Donkeys and other working animals can support transport and farm activities in suitable communities.

Livestock Production Systems

Livestock production systems describe how animals are kept, fed and managed. The choice of system depends on land, climate, available feed, capital, labour and market demand.

1. Pastoral Production System

Pastoralism involves keeping livestock while moving animals between grazing areas and water sources according to seasonal resource availability. It is particularly important in arid and semi-arid environments.

2. Agro-Pastoral Production System

Agro-pastoral systems combine livestock keeping with crop production. Crop residues can sometimes provide animal feed, while properly managed livestock manure can support crop soil fertility.

3. Mixed Crop-Livestock System

Farmers produce crops and keep livestock on the same farm. This allows integration of crop residues, fodder, manure and other resources.

4. Intensive Production System

Animals are kept in controlled housing environments with managed feeding, water supply, health care and production records. Examples include intensive poultry and dairy production.

5. Semi-Intensive Production System

Animals receive a combination of controlled feeding and access to grazing or outdoor areas. This system combines some advantages of intensive and extensive production.

6. Extensive Production System

Animals obtain much of their feed through grazing and browsing over larger areas. It requires suitable grazing resources and responsible land and water management.

Animal Breeding and Genetic Improvement

Animal breeding involves selecting suitable animals for reproduction to improve desirable characteristics in future generations.

Breed selection should consider climate, disease resistance, feed availability, production objectives and the ability of animals to adapt to local conditions.

Important Breeding Considerations

  • Select healthy animals with desirable production characteristics.
  • Choose breeds suited to the local environment.
  • Maintain appropriate breeding records.
  • Prevent uncontrolled inbreeding.
  • Consider fertility, growth rate and productivity.
  • Seek qualified advice when selecting breeding stock.
  • Use artificial insemination where appropriate and professionally supported.

Common Breeding Methods

  • Natural mating: A selected male mates with a female naturally.
  • Artificial insemination: Semen is introduced into the female reproductive tract using a professional technique.
  • Selective breeding: Animals are selected based on desirable inherited traits.
  • Crossbreeding: Different breeds are mated to combine selected characteristics.

Animal Nutrition and Feeding Management

Animal nutrition is essential for growth, reproduction, milk production, meat production, immunity and general animal health.

Animals require appropriate quantities of energy, protein, fibre, minerals, vitamins and clean water. Nutritional needs vary according to species, age, body condition and production stage.

Major Livestock Feed Sources

  • Pasture: Natural and cultivated grasses.
  • Fodder: Napier grass, Rhodes grass and suitable forage crops.
  • Legume fodder: Suitable forage legumes that provide additional protein.
  • Hay: Dried grass or legumes stored for later feeding.
  • Silage: Preserved green fodder stored under suitable airtight conditions.
  • Crop residues: Appropriate residues such as maize stover and cereal straw.
  • Concentrates: Formulated feeds that supply concentrated nutrients.
  • Mineral supplements: Appropriate mineral sources for livestock requirements.

Feeding Management Practices

  • Provide feed suitable for the animal species and production stage.
  • Maintain a reliable supply of clean drinking water.
  • Store feeds in dry and hygienic conditions.
  • Introduce changes in feed gradually.
  • Prevent mouldy or contaminated feed from being consumed.
  • Monitor animal body condition and productivity.
  • Seek professional advice when formulating balanced rations.

Livestock Feed Security

Feed availability is a major challenge during drought. Farmers can improve preparedness by conserving hay, producing suitable fodder, managing grazing areas and planning feed reserves before dry seasons.

Pasture and Fodder Production

Pasture and fodder production provides livestock with important feed resources. Sustainable management helps improve animal nutrition and reduce pressure on natural grazing areas.

Pasture Management

  • Protect grazing areas from excessive grazing.
  • Use rotational grazing where suitable.
  • Allow pasture adequate time to recover.
  • Control invasive weeds and undesirable plants.
  • Maintain suitable ground cover.
  • Protect water points and grazing land from degradation.

Fodder Conservation

Fodder conservation involves storing feed during periods of availability for use when grazing and fresh feed become limited.

  • Harvest suitable grasses at appropriate maturity.
  • Prepare and store hay correctly.
  • Make silage using suitable forage materials.
  • Protect conserved feed from rain, moisture and pests.
  • Keep feed storage facilities clean and secure.

Animal Health and Disease Prevention

Healthy animals are important for profitable and sustainable livestock production. Disease prevention, early detection and access to veterinary services help reduce livestock losses.

Common Livestock Health Challenges

  • Tick-borne diseases.
  • Internal and external parasites.
  • Respiratory infections.
  • Foot and mouth disease.
  • Contagious livestock diseases.
  • Poultry respiratory and digestive diseases.
  • Reproductive health problems.
  • Nutritional deficiencies and metabolic disorders.

The diseases affecting livestock vary by species, location, season and production system. Veterinary professionals should identify suspected diseases and recommend appropriate control measures.

Preventive Animal Health Practices

  • Follow locally recommended vaccination programmes.
  • Arrange regular veterinary inspections.
  • Monitor animals for changes in behaviour and appetite.
  • Isolate sick animals where appropriate.
  • Use parasite control measures under veterinary guidance.
  • Maintain clean feeding and watering equipment.
  • Keep appropriate animal health records.
  • Report suspected serious or unusual disease outbreaks promptly.

Responsible Use of Veterinary Medicines

Veterinary medicines should be used according to professional advice and product instructions. Observe withdrawal periods for meat and milk. Avoid unnecessary antibiotic use and never administer medicines without understanding the correct product, dose and safety requirements.

Livestock Biosecurity

Biosecurity refers to measures used to prevent the introduction and spread of diseases among animals, farms and livestock production facilities.

Important Biosecurity Practices

  • Control unnecessary movement of people and animals into farms.
  • Quarantine newly introduced animals when advised.
  • Clean and disinfect suitable equipment and housing.
  • Separate sick animals from healthy animals.
  • Use clean water and safe feed sources.
  • Manage manure and animal waste responsibly.
  • Control rodents, insects and other disease carriers.
  • Keep accurate animal movement and health records.

Livestock Housing and Animal Welfare

Suitable animal housing protects livestock from extreme weather, predators, injuries and other environmental challenges.

Good Housing Requirements

  • Provide adequate space for the animals.
  • Ensure suitable ventilation and shade.
  • Maintain clean and dry resting areas.
  • Provide proper drainage.
  • Use safe floors and suitable fencing.
  • Provide convenient access to feed and water.
  • Separate animals when required for health or management reasons.

Animal Welfare

Animal welfare means ensuring that animals are healthy, properly fed, comfortable and protected from unnecessary pain, injury, fear and distress.

Proper handling, transportation, housing and access to veterinary care are important parts of responsible livestock management.

Dairy Production and Milk Management

Dairy farming involves keeping animals for milk production. Cattle, goats and camels can all contribute to milk production under suitable production systems.

Important Dairy Management Practices

  • Select animals with suitable dairy characteristics.
  • Provide balanced nutrition and adequate water.
  • Maintain clean and comfortable housing.
  • Follow proper milking hygiene.
  • Monitor udder health and milk quality.
  • Use clean milking equipment.
  • Cool and store milk appropriately.
  • Maintain breeding, health and milk production records.

Milk Hygiene

Milk should be collected using clean hands and equipment, protected from contamination and cooled promptly under appropriate conditions. Safe handling is important for consumer health and product quality.

Beef Production and Management

Beef production involves raising cattle for meat. Successful production depends on breed suitability, nutrition, animal health, housing or grazing management and market requirements.

Beef Production Practices

  • Select suitable beef or dual-purpose animals.
  • Provide adequate grazing and supplementary feed where required.
  • Monitor animal body condition and growth.
  • Maintain appropriate animal health programmes.
  • Provide clean water and suitable shelter.
  • Keep production and financial records.
  • Use approved slaughter and meat handling facilities.

Poultry Production and Management

Poultry farming involves keeping domestic birds for meat, eggs and breeding. It can provide income and nutritious food for households and businesses.

Major Poultry Types

  • Broilers: Chickens raised primarily for meat.
  • Layers: Chickens managed mainly for egg production.
  • Indigenous chickens: Local chickens kept for meat, eggs and household income.
  • Improved indigenous chickens: Selected birds developed for improved production characteristics.
  • Other poultry: Ducks, turkeys, geese and guinea fowl.

Poultry Management Practices

  • Purchase healthy chicks from reliable suppliers.
  • Prepare housing before introducing birds.
  • Provide appropriate brooding temperature for chicks.
  • Supply suitable feed and clean water.
  • Follow recommended vaccination programmes.
  • Maintain good ventilation and litter management.
  • Control rodents and other disease carriers.
  • Keep records of feed consumption, mortality and production.

Camel Production and Management

Camel keeping is an important livestock activity in arid and semi-arid areas. Camels provide milk, meat, transport and income to pastoral communities.

Camel Management Practices

  • Provide access to suitable grazing and browsing resources.
  • Ensure access to safe drinking water.
  • Monitor body condition and general health.
  • Provide veterinary care when needed.
  • Manage breeding and young camel calves appropriately.
  • Maintain hygienic milk handling practices.
  • Protect animals from avoidable injuries and disease.

Importance of Camels in Dryland Areas

Camels are well adapted to many dryland environments and can support pastoral livelihoods. Their production still depends on adequate nutrition, water, animal health services and responsible range management.

Sheep and Goat Production

Sheep and goats are important livestock species for meat, milk, skins and household income. They are widely kept by pastoral and smallholder farmers.

Goat Management

  • Select breeds suited to the local environment and production goals.
  • Provide appropriate browse, pasture and supplementary feed.
  • Maintain clean and secure housing.
  • Monitor parasites and common diseases.
  • Manage breeding and kidding carefully.
  • Provide clean water and mineral supplementation where needed.

Sheep Management

  • Select healthy breeding stock.
  • Provide suitable grazing and supplementary feeding.
  • Monitor body condition and growth.
  • Maintain appropriate parasite control.
  • Provide clean and well-drained housing.
  • Manage lambing and young lamb care.

Livestock Water Management

Water is essential for digestion, temperature regulation, nutrient transport, reproduction and production of milk and meat.

Water Management Practices

  • Provide clean drinking water regularly.
  • Maintain water troughs and storage facilities.
  • Prevent contamination of water sources.
  • Repair leaks and reduce water wastage.
  • Plan water supplies for dry seasons.
  • Monitor water availability as herd sizes change.

Water requirements differ according to animal species, body size, feed composition, production stage and environmental temperature. Farmers should seek appropriate local recommendations.

Livestock Management During Drought

Drought can reduce pasture availability, limit water supplies, weaken animal body condition and increase livestock losses. Planning before drought becomes severe is important.

Drought Preparedness Strategies

  • Monitor rainfall, pasture conditions and water availability.
  • Conserve hay and other suitable animal feeds.
  • Plan access to reliable water sources.
  • Maintain appropriate herd sizes for available resources.
  • Consider timely and responsible destocking where necessary.
  • Prioritize the care of vulnerable and productive animals.
  • Seek veterinary and livestock extension advice.
  • Prepare recovery plans for the period after drought.

Focus on Northern Kenya and Garissa

Livestock Waste Management

Proper livestock waste management helps maintain clean farms, reduce environmental contamination and improve resource efficiency.

Manure Management

  • Collect manure from appropriate housing areas.
  • Compost manure under suitable conditions.
  • Use properly treated manure to improve soil fertility.
  • Prevent waste from entering water sources.
  • Manage animal bedding and wastewater responsibly.
  • Maintain cleanliness around animal shelters.

Biogas Opportunities

Under suitable conditions, livestock manure can be used in biogas systems to produce energy. The remaining digestate may also be used as a soil amendment when properly managed.

Milk Processing

Processing milk into suitable products such as yoghurt, cheese and other dairy products under safe conditions.

Meat Processing

Egg Marketing

Hides and Skins

Honey Production

Livestock Business and Economics

Livestock farming is an agricultural business that requires planning, investment, good management and financial monitoring.

Major Livestock Business Costs

  • Purchase of animals and breeding stock.
  • Housing and farm construction.
  • Animal feed and supplements.
  • Water supply and storage.
  • Veterinary services and medicines.
  • Labour and transport.
  • Equipment and farm maintenance.
  • Marketing and processing expenses.

Livestock Income Sources

  • Sale of live animals.
  • Milk and dairy products.
  • Meat and meat products.
  • Egg production.
  • Breeding stock and young animals.
  • Hides, skins, wool and other products.
  • Honey and beeswax.
  • Manure and compost products.

Livestock Profit Formula

Total Revenue = Total Income from Animal Products and Sales

Total Profit = Total Revenue − Total Production Costs

Farmers should include feed, labour, animal health, water, transport, housing and other operating costs when calculating profitability.
