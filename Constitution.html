ORGANIZATIONAL GOVERNANCE

OUR CONSTITUTION

The governing framework, principles, values and institutional guidelines of GOREALE Agricultural Innovators.

GOREALE AGRICULTURAL INNOVATORS

Organization Type: Community-Based Organization (CBO)

Location: Goreale Ward, Lagdera Constituency, Garissa County, Kenya

Document: Official Organizational Constitution

Adoption Date: __________________________

Registration Number: __________________________

Motto: Cultivating Innovation. Growing Sustainable Futures.

PREAMBLE

We, the members of GOREALE Agricultural Innovators, united by our commitment to sustainable agriculture, community development, innovation, environmental conservation and improved livelihoods, hereby establish this Community-Based Organization.

Recognizing the importance of collective action, agricultural innovation, youth participation, gender inclusion, environmental sustainability and community empowerment, we adopt this Constitution as the governing framework of our organization.

We commit ourselves to working together to advance agricultural development, strengthen community resilience, promote food security and create opportunities for present and future generations.

CHAPTER ONE: PRELIMINARY PROVISIONS

ARTICLE 1: NAME OF THE ORGANIZATION

  1. The organization shall be known as GOREALE AGRICULTURAL INNOVATORS.
  2. The organization shall operate as a Community-Based Organization (CBO).
  3. The organization may hereinafter be referred to as GOREALE or the Organization.

ARTICLE 2: REGISTERED OFFICE AND AREA OF OPERATION

  1. The principal operational base shall be Goreale Ward, Lagdera Constituency, Garissa County, Kenya.
  2. The Organization may undertake activities in other parts of Garissa County, Northeastern Kenya and elsewhere in Kenya, subject to applicable laws.
  3. The Organization may establish operational offices or community coordination points where necessary.

ARTICLE 3: LEGAL STATUS

  1. The Organization shall operate as a non-profit, community-based organization.
  2. Its activities shall be guided by applicable laws and regulations of the Republic of Kenya.
  3. It shall not distribute profits or organizational assets among members for personal benefit.
  4. All resources shall be applied towards achieving its objectives.

ARTICLE 4: VISION

A sustainable, innovative and empowered community where agriculture drives food security, environmental conservation, economic opportunities and improved livelihoods.

ARTICLE 5: MISSION

To advance sustainable agriculture through innovation, climate-smart solutions, agricultural knowledge, environmental conservation and meaningful community participation.

ARTICLE 6: CORE VALUES

The Organization shall be guided by:

  1. Integrity and accountability.
  2. Transparency and good governance.
  3. Innovation and creativity.
  4. Environmental responsibility.
  5. Inclusivity and equality.
  6. Community participation.
  7. Collaboration and partnership.
  8. Sustainability and resilience.
  9. Respect and teamwork.
  10. Professionalism.

CHAPTER TWO: OBJECTIVES AND FUNCTIONS

ARTICLE 7: GENERAL OBJECTIVE

To promote sustainable agricultural development, community empowerment, environmental conservation and improved socioeconomic livelihoods through innovation, knowledge sharing and collective participation.

ARTICLE 8: SPECIFIC OBJECTIVES

  1. Promote climate-smart and sustainable agricultural practices.
  2. Support crop production and diversification.
  3. Improve livestock production and management.
  4. Promote afforestation, agroforestry and environmental restoration.
  5. Encourage soil and water conservation.
  6. Promote agricultural technology and innovation.
  7. Support agricultural research, learning and knowledge exchange.
  8. Promote agribusiness, entrepreneurship and market access.
  9. Strengthen food security and community nutrition.
  10. Encourage youth and women's participation in agriculture.
  11. Promote community development and economic empowerment.
  12. Support climate adaptation, drought preparedness and resilience.
  13. Facilitate agricultural training, demonstrations and capacity building.
  14. Establish partnerships with government institutions, NGOs, development partners, research institutions and private-sector organizations.
  15. Promote sustainable natural resource management.
  16. Encourage community-led initiatives and volunteer participation.

ARTICLE 9: FUNCTIONS

The Organization may:

  1. Conduct agricultural training and awareness programmes.
  2. Establish demonstration farms and community agricultural projects.
  3. Mobilize resources for approved community initiatives.
  4. Organize workshops, field visits and agricultural exhibitions.
  5. Develop and implement community development programmes.
  6. Participate in research and agricultural innovation activities.
  7. Establish partnerships and collaborative programmes.
  8. Prepare reports, proposals and development plans.
  9. Support community members through approved programmes.

CHAPTER THREE: MEMBERSHIP

ARTICLE 10: ELIGIBILITY

  1. Membership shall be open to individuals who support the vision, mission and objectives of the Organization.
  2. Applicants shall agree to abide by this Constitution.
  3. Applicants shall demonstrate willingness to participate in community development.
  4. Membership shall be open without discrimination based on gender, disability, social background or economic status.

ARTICLE 11: CATEGORIES OF MEMBERSHIP

  1. Founding Members: Individuals involved in establishing the Organization.
  2. Ordinary Members: Individuals formally admitted into membership.
  3. Honorary Members: Individuals recognized for exceptional contributions.
  4. Associate Members: Individuals or groups supporting the Organization without necessarily exercising full voting rights.
  5. Youth Members: Eligible young people participating in organizational activities.

ARTICLE 12: RIGHTS OF MEMBERS

  1. Participate in organizational activities.
  2. Attend General Assembly meetings.
  3. Vote, where eligible.
  4. Contest leadership positions, subject to eligibility requirements.
  5. Access organizational information and approved reports.
  6. Present proposals and recommendations.
  7. Receive fair and equal treatment.
  8. Participate in decision-making processes.

ARTICLE 13: RESPONSIBILITIES OF MEMBERS

  1. Respect and uphold this Constitution.
  2. Support the vision and objectives of the Organization.
  3. Participate in meetings and activities.
  4. Protect organizational property and resources.
  5. Promote unity, transparency and accountability.
  6. Avoid conduct that damages the reputation of the Organization.
  7. Fulfil approved membership obligations.
  8. Respect decisions lawfully made by the Organization.

ARTICLE 14: ADMISSION AND TERMINATION OF MEMBERSHIP

  1. Applications for membership shall be submitted to the Secretary or designated membership officer.
  2. Admission shall be approved in accordance with procedures established by the Organization.
  3. Membership may cease through voluntary resignation, death, expulsion or other lawful grounds.
  4. A member shall be given an opportunity to respond before disciplinary termination.
  5. Any member may appeal a membership decision through the General Assembly.

CHAPTER FOUR: GOVERNANCE AND LEADERSHIP

ARTICLE 15: GOVERNING STRUCTURE

The Organization shall have the following governance organs:

  1. General Assembly.
  2. Executive Committee.
  3. Subcommittees and project committees, where necessary.

ARTICLE 16: GENERAL ASSEMBLY

  1. The General Assembly shall be the supreme decision-making organ.
  2. It shall consist of all eligible members.
  3. It shall approve organizational policies, plans and budgets.
  4. It shall elect and remove office bearers in accordance with this Constitution.
  5. It shall receive and consider annual reports.
  6. It shall approve constitutional amendments.
  7. It shall provide overall direction and oversight.

ARTICLE 17: EXECUTIVE COMMITTEE

The Executive Committee shall consist of:

  1. Chairperson.
  2. Vice Chairperson.
  3. Secretary.
  4. Assistant Secretary.
  5. Treasurer.
  6. Organizing Secretary.
  7. Additional committee members as approved by the General Assembly.

ARTICLE 18: DUTIES OF THE CHAIRPERSON

  1. Provide overall leadership.
  2. Preside over General Assembly and Executive Committee meetings.
  3. Represent the Organization in official engagements.
  4. Ensure implementation of approved decisions.
  5. Promote accountability and effective governance.

ARTICLE 19: DUTIES OF THE VICE CHAIRPERSON

  1. Assist the Chairperson.
  2. Perform the Chairperson's duties in their absence.
  3. Undertake responsibilities assigned by the Executive Committee.

ARTICLE 20: DUTIES OF THE SECRETARY

  1. Maintain official records and correspondence.
  2. Prepare and distribute meeting notices.
  3. Record and maintain meeting minutes.
  4. Maintain membership records.
  5. Coordinate administrative communication.
  6. Prepare organizational reports with relevant officers.

ARTICLE 21: DUTIES OF THE ASSISTANT SECRETARY

  1. Assist the Secretary.
  2. Maintain records when delegated.
  3. Perform the Secretary's duties in their absence.

ARTICLE 22: DUTIES OF THE TREASURER

  1. Manage organizational financial records.
  2. Maintain records of income and expenditure.
  3. Present financial reports.
  4. Ensure proper documentation of transactions.
  5. Support preparation of annual budgets.
  6. Safeguard organizational financial information.
  7. Ensure funds are used for approved purposes.

ARTICLE 23: DUTIES OF THE ORGANIZING SECRETARY

  1. Coordinate organizational programmes and events.
  2. Mobilize members for approved activities.
  3. Support community engagement.
  4. Coordinate logistical arrangements.
  5. Assist in monitoring implementation of activities.

ARTICLE 24: ELECTIONS AND TENURE

  1. Office bearers shall be elected democratically by eligible members.
  2. Elections shall be conducted through transparent and fair procedures.
  3. The tenure of office shall be three years.
  4. Office bearers may be re-elected.
  5. Elections shall be conducted in accordance with procedures approved by the General Assembly.
  6. Vacancies shall be filled through an approved election or interim appointment until the next General Assembly.

ARTICLE 25: REMOVAL FROM OFFICE

An office bearer may be removed for:

  1. Gross misconduct.
  2. Misappropriation of organizational funds.
  3. Abuse of office.
  4. Persistent failure to perform assigned responsibilities.
  5. Violation of this Constitution.
  6. Conduct seriously prejudicial to the Organization.

Removal shall require a fair hearing and approval by the General Assembly in accordance with this Constitution.

CHAPTER FIVE: MEETINGS

ARTICLE 26: ANNUAL GENERAL MEETING

  1. The Organization shall hold an Annual General Meeting once every calendar year.
  2. The meeting shall review organizational performance, finances and programmes.
  3. It shall approve annual plans and budgets.
  4. It shall receive reports from the Executive Committee.

ARTICLE 27: ORDINARY MEETINGS

The Executive Committee shall meet at least once every three months or as necessary.

ARTICLE 28: SPECIAL GENERAL MEETINGS

A Special General Meeting may be convened by the Chairperson or upon a written request by at least one-third of eligible members.

ARTICLE 29: NOTICE OF MEETINGS

  1. Ordinary General Meetings shall receive at least fourteen days' notice.
  2. Special meetings shall receive reasonable notice, ordinarily not less than seven days.
  3. Emergency meetings may be convened where circumstances require.

ARTICLE 30: QUORUM

  1. The quorum for a General Assembly meeting shall be one-half of eligible members.
  2. The quorum for Executive Committee meetings shall be a simple majority of its serving members.
  3. If quorum is not achieved, the meeting shall be adjourned and reconvened according to procedures approved by the Organization.

ARTICLE 31: DECISION-MAKING

  1. Decisions shall preferably be made through consensus.
  2. Where consensus is not achieved, decisions shall be made through voting.
  3. Each eligible voting member shall have one vote.
  4. In the event of a tie, the Chairperson shall exercise a casting vote, where applicable.

CHAPTER SIX: FINANCIAL MANAGEMENT

ARTICLE 32: SOURCES OF FUNDS

The Organization may obtain funds through:

  1. Membership contributions and subscriptions.
  2. Donations and voluntary contributions.
  3. Grants from government institutions.
  4. Support from NGOs and development partners.
  5. Corporate and institutional partnerships.
  6. Fundraising activities.
  7. Income from approved organizational projects.
  8. Other lawful sources consistent with its objectives.

ARTICLE 33: BANK ACCOUNT

  1. The Organization shall maintain an account in its official name.
  2. All organizational funds shall be deposited into the approved account.
  3. Authorized signatories shall be appointed by the General Assembly.
  4. Withdrawals shall require authorization by at least two approved signatories, in accordance with the financial policy.
  5. Personal accounts shall not be used to hold organizational funds except in exceptional circumstances formally approved and documented by the Executive Committee.

ARTICLE 34: FINANCIAL YEAR

The financial year shall run from 1 January to 31 December unless otherwise approved by the General Assembly or required by applicable regulations.

ARTICLE 35: BUDGET AND EXPENDITURE

  1. The Organization shall prepare an annual budget.
  2. Expenditure shall be consistent with approved objectives and budgets.
  3. All transactions shall be supported by appropriate documentation.
  4. No member shall use organizational resources for unauthorized personal benefit.

ARTICLE 36: FINANCIAL RECORDS AND AUDIT

  1. Proper books of accounts shall be maintained.
  2. Financial statements shall be presented annually.
  3. The General Assembly shall appoint an independent auditor or qualified reviewer where appropriate.
  4. Financial records shall be available for authorized inspection.

CHAPTER SEVEN: PROJECTS, PARTNERSHIPS AND COLLABORATION

ARTICLE 37: PROJECT IMPLEMENTATION

The Organization may design and implement programmes relating to:

  1. Climate-smart agriculture.
  2. Crop production.
  3. Livestock development.
  4. Afforestation and agroforestry.
  5. Soil and water conservation.
  6. Agricultural technology.
  7. Research and innovation.
  8. Agribusiness and entrepreneurship.
  9. Food security.
  10. Community development and participation.

ARTICLE 38: PARTNERSHIPS

  1. The Organization may collaborate with government institutions, NGOs, community groups, private-sector entities and development partners.
  2. Partnerships shall advance the Organization's objectives.
  3. Agreements shall be documented and approved by the appropriate governing organ.
  4. No partnership shall compromise the independence, integrity or non-profit character of the Organization.

ARTICLE 39: ACCOUNTABILITY TO PARTNERS AND BENEFICIARIES

  1. Maintain transparency in project implementation.
  2. Prepare progress and financial reports.
  3. Promote community participation.
  4. Ensure responsible utilization of resources.
  5. Monitor and evaluate project outcomes.

CHAPTER EIGHT: ETHICS, DISCIPLINE AND CONFLICT RESOLUTION

ARTICLE 40: CODE OF CONDUCT

All members and office bearers shall:

  1. Demonstrate integrity and honesty.
  2. Avoid corruption and misuse of resources.
  3. Respect other members.
  4. Avoid discrimination and harassment.
  5. Declare conflicts of interest.
  6. Protect confidential organizational information.
  7. Uphold the reputation and objectives of the Organization.

ARTICLE 41: DISCIPLINARY PROCEDURES

  1. Complaints shall be submitted to the Executive Committee.
  2. The affected member shall be informed of the allegations.
  3. The member shall be given an opportunity to respond.
  4. Disciplinary action shall be proportionate to the misconduct.
  5. A member shall have the right to appeal to the General Assembly.

ARTICLE 42: CONFLICT RESOLUTION

Disputes shall first be resolved through internal dialogue and mediation.

Where internal resolution fails, the Organization may refer the matter to an appropriate mediation mechanism or lawful authority.

CHAPTER NINE: AMENDMENTS AND DISSOLUTION

ARTICLE 43: CONSTITUTIONAL AMENDMENTS

  1. Any member may propose an amendment.
  2. Proposed amendments shall be submitted in writing to the Secretary.
  3. Members shall receive reasonable notice of proposed amendments.
  4. Amendments shall require approval by at least two-thirds of eligible members present and voting at a properly constituted General Assembly.
  5. Amendments shall comply with applicable registration and regulatory requirements.

ARTICLE 44: DISSOLUTION

  1. The Organization may be dissolved by a resolution supported by at least two-thirds of eligible members at a Special General Meeting convened for that purpose.
  2. All outstanding liabilities shall be settled.
  3. Remaining assets shall not be distributed among members.
  4. Remaining assets shall be transferred to another registered non-profit organization with similar objectives, subject to applicable law and regulatory approval.
  5. Dissolution shall be reported to the relevant authorities in accordance with applicable requirements.

CHAPTER TEN: MISCELLANEOUS PROVISIONS

ARTICLE 45: SEAL AND OFFICIAL DOCUMENTS

The Organization may adopt an official stamp, letterhead, logo and other identification materials for administrative and institutional purposes.

ARTICLE 46: POLICIES AND PROCEDURES

The Executive Committee may propose operational policies, including financial, procurement, human resource, safeguarding and project management policies, subject to approval by the General Assembly where required.

ARTICLE 47: INTERPRETATION

Any question regarding the interpretation of this Constitution shall initially be considered by the Executive Committee, subject to the authority of the General Assembly and applicable laws.

ARTICLE 48: SUPREMACY OF THE CONSTITUTION

This Constitution shall be the principal governing document of the Organization. All organizational activities and decisions shall be consistent with its provisions and the applicable laws of Kenya.

ADOPTION AND DECLARATION

We, the undersigned founding members of GOREALE Agricultural Innovators, hereby declare that we have read, understood and adopted this Constitution as the governing document of our Community-Based Organization.
