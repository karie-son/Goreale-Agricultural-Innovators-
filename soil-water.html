Protecting Our Most Valuable Resources

Soil and water are essential resources for agricultural production, food security and environmental sustainability. Healthy soil supports plant growth, stores nutrients and holds moisture, while water supports crops, livestock and farming communities.

Soil and water conservation involves protecting land against erosion, improving soil fertility, collecting rainwater and using available water efficiently. These practices help farmers maintain productive land and become more resilient to droughts and changing rainfall patterns.

Goreale Agricultural Innovators promotes practical conservation methods that can be adapted to different farming systems, including smallholder farms and dryland areas.

Our Key Areas of Focus

1. Soil Conservation

Protecting topsoil from erosion, degradation and nutrient loss through good land management and protective ground cover.

2. Water Harvesting

Collecting and storing rainwater for crop irrigation, livestock watering and other productive agricultural uses.

3. Soil Fertility Management

Improving soil nutrients and organic matter through compost, manure, crop rotation and appropriate fertiliser use.

4. Erosion Control

Reducing the movement of soil by water and wind using terraces, grass strips, contour farming and vegetation.

5. Efficient Irrigation

Promoting water-saving irrigation systems and careful scheduling to reduce water losses and improve crop production.

6. Watershed Protection

Protecting catchments, riverbanks, wetlands and other natural water sources through responsible land management.

Understanding Soil Health

Soil health refers to the ability of soil to function as a living ecosystem that supports plants, animals and agricultural production. Good soil contains organic matter, nutrients, air, water and beneficial organisms.

Soil Organic Matter

Compost, farmyard manure and decomposed crop residues improve soil structure, nutrient availability and water retention.

Soil Testing

Soil testing helps farmers understand nutrient levels and soil conditions before choosing fertilisers or soil amendments.

Crop Rotation

Rotating crops, including suitable legumes, can improve soil fertility, diversify production and help manage certain pests and diseases.

Mulching

Covering soil with crop residues, dry grass or suitable organic materials reduces evaporation and protects the soil surface.

Cover Crops

Growing suitable plants between main cropping seasons protects bare soil, improves ground cover and can contribute organic matter.

Minimum Soil Disturbance

Reducing unnecessary tillage can help maintain soil structure and reduce exposure to erosion when combined with appropriate cover and crop rotation.

Soil Erosion and Its Control

Soil erosion occurs when topsoil is removed by water, wind or human activities. It reduces land productivity, damages farm structures and can contribute to sedimentation in rivers and water reservoirs.

Common Types of Soil Erosion

Sheet Erosion

The gradual removal of a thin layer of soil across a wide area by surface runoff.

Rill Erosion

Small channels formed when flowing rainwater concentrates and carries soil downhill.

Gully Erosion

Large channels develop when runoff repeatedly flows through the same area, removing significant amounts of soil.

Wind Erosion

Strong winds carry away loose, dry soil, especially where land is bare and vegetation is limited.

Riverbank Erosion

Flowing water gradually removes soil from riverbanks, especially where natural vegetation has been cleared.

Practical Soil Erosion Control Methods

  • Plant crops along contour lines rather than directly downhill.
  • Establish terraces on suitable sloping land.
  • Maintain grass strips and vegetation barriers.
  • Use mulch and crop residues to protect bare soil.
  • Plant trees and suitable cover crops.
  • Protect riverbanks and avoid cultivation too close to waterways.
  • Control livestock grazing on vulnerable land.
  • Maintain drainage channels and erosion-control structures.

Important Practice

Conservation structures must be designed according to the slope, soil type and expected water flow. Poorly designed drains or terraces can redirect runoff and cause erosion elsewhere. Seek advice from an agricultural or soil conservation officer before constructing major structures.

Rainwater Harvesting and Storage

Rainwater harvesting involves collecting and storing rainfall or surface runoff for future use. It is particularly valuable in areas where rainfall is seasonal or unreliable.

Roof Catchment

Collecting rainwater from roofs through gutters and directing it into properly covered storage tanks.

Water Pans and Farm Ponds

Constructing suitable storage structures to collect runoff for livestock, irrigation and other farm activities.

Earth Dams

Storing surface runoff in appropriately designed earth reservoirs for use during dry periods.

Contour Bunds

Building barriers across slopes to slow runoff and encourage more water to enter the soil.

Zai Pits and Planting Pits

Small planting basins that collect runoff around crops and can be improved with suitable organic matter.

Rock Catchment

Collecting runoff from suitable rock surfaces and directing it into storage facilities.

Safe Water Storage

  • Use suitable, clean and covered storage tanks.
  • Install screens to reduce entry of leaves and debris.
  • Provide safe overflow channels.
  • Inspect storage structures for leaks and damage.
  • Prevent livestock and other contamination around water sources.
  • Treat harvested water appropriately if it will be used for drinking.

Efficient Irrigation Management

Irrigation supports crop production when rainfall is insufficient. However, water must be applied carefully to avoid unnecessary losses, waterlogging, nutrient leaching and soil salinity.

Drip Irrigation

Delivers water slowly near plant roots through pipes and emitters. It is useful for vegetables, fruit trees and other crops when properly maintained.

Sprinkler Irrigation

Distributes water through sprinklers. Proper design and scheduling help reduce losses from wind and evaporation.

Surface Irrigation

Uses channels or furrows to move water across agricultural land. Good land levelling and flow management are important.

Good Irrigation Practices

  • Choose an irrigation method suitable for the crop and soil.
  • Water according to crop needs and soil moisture conditions.
  • Prefer cooler parts of the day where practical.
  • Repair leaking pipes, valves and irrigation equipment.
  • Use mulch to reduce evaporation around crops.
  • Monitor soil salinity where irrigation water quality is a concern.
  • Avoid over-irrigation and unnecessary waterlogging.

Soil and Water Conservation in Dryland Areas

Dryland agriculture faces challenges such as irregular rainfall, high evaporation, water shortages, land degradation and drought. Farmers in northern Kenya, including Garissa County, can benefit from conservation methods that capture rainfall and protect soil moisture.

Practical Options for Garissa and Northern Kenya

  • Harvest rainwater from roofs and suitable catchment surfaces.
  • Use water pans and storage tanks where technically suitable.
  • Apply mulch to reduce evaporation from cultivated soil.
  • Establish planting basins and suitable micro-catchments.
  • Grow drought-tolerant crops suited to local conditions.
  • Plant suitable indigenous trees and drought-adapted vegetation.
  • Protect grazing land through planned grazing and resting periods.
  • Maintain water points and manage livestock access carefully.

Conservation methods should be selected according to local soil, rainfall, terrain, water availability and community needs. A method suitable for one farm may not be suitable for another.

Benefits of Soil and Water Conservation

Improved Soil Fertility

Protects valuable topsoil and supports better nutrient management and long-term land productivity.

Better Water Availability

Increases the amount of water retained in soil and makes harvested water available during dry periods.

Higher Farm Resilience

Helps farming systems cope with drought, heavy rainfall and changing weather conditions.

Reduced Land Degradation

Limits soil erosion, protects vegetation and reduces damage to agricultural land.

Improved Food Security

Supports more reliable crop and livestock production through responsible resource management.

Environmental Protection

Helps protect rivers, wetlands, catchments and other natural resources from degradation.

Our Approach to Conservation

Goreale Agricultural Innovators encourages practical, affordable and community-supported solutions. Successful conservation requires planning, local knowledge, technical guidance and regular maintenance.

1. Assess the Land

Understand soil type, slope, erosion risks, vegetation cover and available water sources.

2. Identify Priorities

Identify the main challenges affecting the farm or community and select suitable conservation measures.

3. Plan and Implement

Work with farmers and technical officers to introduce appropriate soil and water conservation practices.

4. Train Farmers

Share knowledge through demonstrations, field activities and practical agricultural education.

5. Monitor Progress

Check soil condition, water availability, erosion control and the performance of conservation structures.

6. Improve Continuously

Maintain structures, learn from results and adjust conservation methods as conditions change.
