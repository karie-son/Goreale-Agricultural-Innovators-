Understanding Climate-Smart Agriculture

Climate-smart agriculture (CSA) is an integrated approach to agricultural development that helps farmers produce food sustainably while responding to the challenges of climate change. It promotes farming systems that improve productivity, strengthen resilience and contribute to environmental protection.

Agriculture depends heavily on natural resources such as land, water, soil and favourable weather conditions. However, changing rainfall patterns, prolonged droughts, rising temperatures, floods, soil degradation and emerging pests and diseases increasingly threaten agricultural production and rural livelihoods.

Climate-smart agriculture encourages farmers to adopt practical technologies and farming methods that respond to these challenges. It combines scientific knowledge, local experience, research, innovation and responsible management of natural resources.

The approach is relevant to crop production, livestock management, forestry, fisheries and wider agricultural value chains. Its practices should be selected according to local climate conditions, available resources, farming systems and community needs.

For smallholder farmers, climate-smart agriculture can provide opportunities to improve food production, protect agricultural investments, reduce avoidable losses and strengthen household food security.

The Three Pillars of Climate-Smart Agriculture

Climate-smart agriculture is guided by three connected objectives that support sustainable agricultural development.

01

Sustainable Productivity

Increasing agricultural production and farmers' incomes while maintaining soil fertility, protecting natural resources and improving the efficiency of agricultural inputs.

02

Climate Adaptation and Resilience

Strengthening the ability of farmers, crops, livestock and agricultural communities to prepare for, withstand and recover from climate-related challenges such as droughts, floods and extreme temperatures.

03

Climate Change Mitigation

Supporting practices that reduce agricultural greenhouse-gas emissions or increase carbon storage in soils and vegetation, where practical and appropriate.

Climate-Smart Farming Practices

Farmers can use different methods and technologies to improve production, conserve resources and manage climate risks.

1. Conservation Agriculture

Reducing unnecessary soil disturbance, maintaining soil cover and practising crop rotation to protect soil structure, moisture and fertility.

2. Water Harvesting

Collecting and storing rainwater for agricultural use through water pans, storage tanks, farm ponds and other suitable harvesting systems.

3. Efficient Irrigation

Using drip irrigation and other efficient irrigation methods to deliver water where crops need it and minimise unnecessary water losses.

4. Drought-Tolerant Crops

Selecting suitable crop varieties that can perform better under limited water conditions and changing weather patterns.

5. Agroforestry

Integrating suitable trees and shrubs into farming systems to provide shade, improve soil protection, support biodiversity and diversify farm products.

6. Crop Diversification

Growing different crops to spread production risks, improve household nutrition and reduce dependence on a single crop.

7. Soil Conservation

Applying mulching, contour farming, cover crops and erosion-control measures to protect agricultural land and maintain soil productivity.

8. Climate-Smart Livestock

Improving animal feeding, water supply, housing, animal health and pasture management to strengthen livestock productivity and resilience.

9. Integrated Pest Management

Combining monitoring, biological control, cultural methods and responsible pesticide use to manage pests and diseases effectively.

10. Weather Information

Using weather forecasts and seasonal climate information to guide planting dates, irrigation, harvesting and other farm decisions.

11. Organic Soil Improvement

Using compost, animal manure and suitable organic materials to improve soil structure, nutrient availability and water retention.

12. Renewable Energy

Exploring suitable solar-powered irrigation, solar drying and other renewable-energy technologies to improve farm operations.

Benefits of Climate-Smart Agriculture

Adopting appropriate climate-smart practices can create economic, environmental and social benefits for farmers and agricultural communities.

Improved Food Security

More reliable agricultural production can improve household access to food and support community nutrition.

Better Farm Productivity

Efficient use of land, water, seeds and other inputs can improve farm performance and productivity.

Climate Resilience

Diversified farming systems and better resource management can help farmers manage climate risks.

Soil Protection

Conservation practices help reduce erosion, maintain soil cover and protect long-term fertility.

Water Conservation

Rainwater harvesting and efficient irrigation support more responsible water use.

Improved Farm Income

Better production planning, reduced losses and diversified products can create income opportunities.

Environmental Protection

Tree planting, soil conservation and responsible resource management support healthier ecosystems.

Knowledge and Innovation

Training, research and technology help farmers make informed agricultural decisions.

Climate-Smart Agriculture in Kenya

Supporting Farmers in Dryland and Arid Areas

Climate variability presents important challenges to agricultural communities across Kenya, particularly in arid and semi-arid areas where rainfall can be low, irregular and difficult to predict.

In areas such as Garissa and other dryland regions, climate-smart agriculture can support farmers and livestock keepers through improved water management, drought-tolerant crops, pasture conservation, agroforestry and sustainable livestock production.

Practical interventions may include rainwater harvesting, water-efficient irrigation, appropriate fodder production, soil-moisture conservation, rangeland management and access to reliable weather information.

Local communities should participate in identifying solutions that match their environmental conditions, available resources, traditional knowledge and agricultural priorities.

Climate-smart agriculture is not a single farming technique. It is a flexible approach that encourages farmers, researchers, agricultural institutions and communities to work together towards more productive and resilient agricultural systems.

How Farmers Can Adopt Climate-Smart Agriculture

Successful adoption starts with understanding local challenges and selecting practices that are practical and suitable for each farm.

Step 1: Assess Farm Conditions

Examine soil quality, water availability, rainfall, crops, livestock and existing agricultural challenges.

Step 2: Identify Climate Risks

Understand possible risks such as drought, flooding, high temperatures, pests, diseases and soil erosion.

Step 3: Select Suitable Practices

Choose appropriate methods based on local conditions, farmer resources, production goals and environmental needs.

Step 4: Build Agricultural Knowledge

Participate in farmer training, demonstrations, agricultural research and extension programmes.

Step 5: Implement and Monitor

Apply selected practices, observe their performance and make improvements based on results and experience.

Step 6: Share Knowledge and Innovations

Exchange experiences with other farmers and agricultural stakeholders to encourage continuous learning and improvement.
